This file is the single authoritative statement of how the four chapters connect. "Canonical" means it is the current record, not a frozen commitment, and authority runs in two directions. Across documents, at any moment: when a cross-chapter claim in a chapter file diverges from this map, the map wins, and the divergence is fixed. ## The chain in one paragraph

Chapter 1 reviews the traditions in environmental politics and policy
(economic, political economy, and institutional lenses), shows that they
treat environmental and social policy as separate domains, presents the
Eco-Social State literature as the integrating move, identifies its two
gaps (no "worlds of the eco-social state" typology; near-exclusive focus on
advanced capitalist democracies), and derives a framework of three
operationalizable dimensions for frontier settings. Chapter 2 demonstrates
historically that these dimensions are constitutive of Amazonian
environmental governance, develops the two-arenas contrast and the
eco-social arena concept with its two mechanisms, and justifies the period
of interest. Chapter 3 conducts a PRISMA systematic review of the last
~10 years of causal research on the causes of Amazon deforestation
(quantitative and qualitative causal claims), maps the causes through the
Chapter 1 lenses, and formalizes the literature's theoretical assumptions
as DAGs — the state of the art on causal inference for this object.
Chapter 4 then tests, descriptively and causally, whether and under what
conditions social policy shapes deforestation directly or by conditioning
the effectiveness of environmental enforcement, positioned against the
gaps Chapter 3 identifies.

## The three operationalizable dimensions (derived in Ch1, applied in Ch2, operationalized in Ch4)

1. **Distributive conflict around environment.** The local political economy
   of environmental exploitation/regulation: who bears the costs of
   conservation, who captures the rents of degradation.
2. **Social conditions of state action.** Inequality, labor structure, and
   welfare provision as determinants of how individuals and communities
   respond to state action in other policy realms.
3. **Multilevel governance.** The institutional arrangements through which
   environmental policy is made and enforced across levels of government,
   and the resulting subnational variation in enforcement.

## Chapter 2's central constructs (produced there, consumed downstream)

- **Two-phase macro-arc.** Phase 1: state-led environmental exploitation
  with regulated exclusion of frontier labor (c. 1870–1985). Phase 2:
  environmental protection and social inclusion built simultaneously from
  the 1988 critical juncture, in separate arenas that never integrated
  (1988–2022).
- **Two-arenas contrast.** Both arenas are federally regulated; the contrast
  is the territorial distribution of costs and benefits. Social policy:
  central regulation plus aligned local incentives → territorial
  convergence. Environmental enforcement: central regulation imposing
  concentrated local costs with dispersed benefits → territorial
  unevenness and contestation.
- **Eco-social arena, mechanisms A and B.** A — cushioning as compensation:
  social protection absorbs the costs of regulation, creates local stakes
  in conservation, strengthens enforcement. B — cushioning as forbearance:
  social vulnerability licenses non-enforcement, weakening the
  environmental state. Historical claim: without integration, A remained
  marginal and B operated by default.
- **Asymmetric resilience** (2016–2022) as the observable implication and
  hypothesis test: environmental apparatus drifted; the social track,
  defended by mass constituencies, was structurally preserved.

## Chapter 3's deliverables (the state of the art)

- A PRISMA-documented corpus of the last ~10 years of causal research on
  Amazon deforestation (window adjustable per initial results; causal
  claims only, quantitative or qualitative).
- A typology of the causes identified in that literature, classified
  through the Chapter 1 lenses.
- An inventory of designs and identification strategies already applied to
  this object.
- DAGs formalizing the theoretical assumptions of the reviewed literature,
  including conflicts between studies' implied causal structures.
- The gaps the dissertation addresses — in particular, whether and how
  social policy figures in this literature as a studied cause or condition.

## Chapter 4's hypotheses (the testable form of the chain)

- **H1.** Social conditions and social policy have a direct effect on
  deforestation; the comparative literature leaves the sign open.
- **H2.** Social-policy coverage moderates the effect of environmental
  enforcement (the eco-social synergy hypothesis; the empirical form of
  the A-versus-B question).
- **H3.** The moderation is itself conditioned by electoral and
  institutional context.

The leading operationalization of H2 is Bolsa Família coverage and social expenditures conditioning
the effect of PPCDAm-era enforcement on municipal deforestation, but the
operationalization is not fixed in advance: Chapter 4's guidance maintains a
map of candidate sources of identifying variation, and design choice follows
the structure of treatment variation — informed by Chapter 3's inventory of
what has already been tried — not a prior commitment.
