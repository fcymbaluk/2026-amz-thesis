# 2026-10-18 — Chapter 1 reordered by lens, the three dimensions repartitioned, the asset typology adopted

**Context.** The supervisor review of August 2026 asked Chapter 1 to
argue the move from the defense of the eco-social perspective to that
perspective's gaps (checklist item 2), and to check whether a paper on
"fossil assets; vulnerable assets" could help (item 4). The introduction
opened on 2026-10-17 with two puzzles and a dependence argument that
Chapters 1 and 2 were to absorb. Chapter 2's codebook was waiting on the
Chapter 1 and 2 guidance, because its deductive codes derive from the
three operationalizable dimensions, and the dimensions as drafted
overlapped (dimension 1 "captured by social indicators", dimension 2
including welfare and regulation, dimension 3 including enforcement), so
the same evidence would have coded to two dimensions.

**Decision.** Four changes, recorded in `ch1-theoretical/GUIDANCE.md`,
`ch2-historical/GUIDANCE.md`, `docs/dissertation-map.md` and
`ch0-introduction/GUIDANCE.md`.

1. The narrative review is ordered by analytical lens (economic:
   Hardin, Olson, EKC; political economy: Boyce and lineage;
   institutional: Ostrom, Environmental State, Eco-Social State), with
   chronology kept inside each block. The earlier arc was chronological
   across the whole review.
2. The analytical threads are three questions asked of every theory:
   lens (which includes what the theory takes well-being to be), level
   and range (unit of analysis and scope, kept apart), and locus of
   institutions. The earlier "environmental–social relationship" thread
   becomes the well-being question inside the lens thread.
3. A new section, "The defense of the eco-social perspective", sits
   between the review and the gap section. It defends the perspective on
   the three threads (and through the Lakatosian criterion if the
   epistemological section keeps it), and it borrows from political
   economy the distributive content the perspective lacks: an asset
   typology, the author's construction on Colgan, Green and Hale (2021),
   with the paper's axis (degradation-forcing versus forest-dependent
   assets) and a second axis of the author's (rent assets versus
   livelihood assets, by whether the loss is marginal or existential for
   the holder). The livelihood row covers the whole population whose
   well-being depends, directly or indirectly, on the degrading activity.
   The introduction's dependence distinction is restated in these terms.
4. The three operationalizable dimensions are partitioned by the kind of
   question each asks: distributive conflict (who gains and who pays,
   read through the asset typology), institutional configuration (what
   rules and provisions exist; renamed from "social conditions of state
   action"), multilevel governance (how rules are made and applied across
   levels of government). The line between dimensions 2 and 3 is the
   line between a rule and its enforcement.

The puzzle the author stated as basic and specific is recorded as two
layers of the theoretical puzzle (the specific one being the
introduction's institutional layer, which Chapter 1 answers); the
empirical Amazon puzzle is unchanged.

**Rationale.** Ordering by lens makes the first thread and the order of
exposition coincide; the cost is separating Hardin from Ostrom's reply,
which the guidance instructs the draft to bridge. The asset typology is
placed in the defense section rather than as a fourth thread because the
threads are questions asked of others' theories and the typology is the
author's answer, and because it is the splice of political economy
(holders, power from holdings) with the eco-social claim (protection can
decouple the livelihood row from the degrading activity). A fourth thread
on well-being was considered and rejected as redundant once the lens
thread records each theory's conception of well-being, which the defense
needs theory by theory. The author's third asset was not named
"social-vulnerable" because in the source "vulnerable" names an asset's
exposure to climate change, not the holder's social condition; the
two-by-two with the paper's axis kept was preferred to three ad hoc
categories because it answers why these three and shows the fourth cell
(forest-dependent rent assets) exists in the Amazon too. The dimensions
were repartitioned so that codes are exclusive; the renaming of dimension
2 changes no downstream hand-off in substance, since welfare provision
stays inside it.

**Consequences.** Chapter 1's checklist item 4 is ticked (the paper is
identified and absorbed); item 2 points to the defense section and closes
when the draft carries it. Chapter 2's dimension questions, purpose
paragraph and codebook checklist item use the new names and prefixes
(`d1-`, `d2-`, `d3-`), and the two-arenas contrast gains Wilson (1980)
via Colgan, Green and Hale as a source. The map's dimension section,
hand-off rows and correspondence table are updated. References to add in
Zotero before drafting: Colgan, Green and Hale 2021; Aklin and
Mildenberger 2020; Wilson 1980; Doyal and Gough 1991; Gough 2017. Chapter
4's guidance still says "social conditions" in its hypotheses text and is
not changed here; its wording follows when that chapter is next revised.
The codebook is built next, from the revised Chapter 2 text.
