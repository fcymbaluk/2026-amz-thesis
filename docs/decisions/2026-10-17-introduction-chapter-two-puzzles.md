# 2026-10-17 — An introduction chapter opens the dissertation with two puzzles and the dependence argument

**Context.** The Quarto book had front matter and four chapters but no
introduction, and the dissertation's framing lived only in the chapter
guidance files. In October 2026 the author drafted an introduction that
opens with two puzzles, one theoretical (how environmental concerns
interact with social well-being, beyond income) and one empirical (how
Amazon deforestation fell between 2004 and 2012 alongside growth and
inclusion, and why it reversed), and added an idea not yet present in any
chapter: on the frontier, livelihoods depend on deforestation-linked
activities but the protective institutions that reached it do not, the
opposite of Europe's growth-dependent welfare states.

**Decision.** The introduction becomes a chapter of the book with its own
folder, `ch0-introduction/` (guidance plus notes), and its own file,
`manuscript/introduction.qmd`, listed after `index.qmd` as an unnumbered
chapter so Chapter 1 keeps its number. The author's draft is migrated,
with each passage marked for development turned into a `TODO-develop`
comment that points to proposed content in the guidance file. The
dependence argument (livelihood dependence versus institutional
dependence) is recorded in the guidance and in `docs/dissertation-map.md`
as the introduction's own idea, to be absorbed by Chapters 1, 2 and 4 as
they develop. The introduction poses the A-versus-B question of the
eco-social arena and does not answer it; Chapter 4 does.

**Rationale.** Opening with the puzzle gives the four chapters a single
argument: the frontier is where the theoretical puzzle takes its hardest
form (well-being and conservation do not align by default) and where the
institutional configuration the eco-social literature assumes does not
hold. A `ch0-` folder keeps the chapter-number grammar of
`docs/repo-conventions.md` and sorts first; a plain `introduction/` folder
was the alternative and was not chosen because every other chapter folder
carries its number. Numbering the introduction was rejected because the
chapters are cited by number across the guidance files. The nine
development notes on the draft were put to the author the same day:
notes 1 to 6 and 8 were approved and applied to the text under the
writing rules (the frontier governed for capital and unprotected for
labor, in place of "total laissez-faire"; cushioning posed as the
A-versus-B question; Hardin and Olson, Rockström and Raworth named; the
social dimension as cause and victim rather than "overlooked"; both
Northern examples, coal regions and the Yellow Vests, with the
production-consumption difference stated; needs theory for well-being and
Polanyi for decommodification; the Amazon literature as two sets of
causes, stated as Chapter 3's finding). Note 7 (the term for the Latin
American welfare model) was deferred to Chapter 1's checklist item 3.
Note 9 (the closing sections) stays a plan in the guidance. Paragraphs
the author did not ask to revise keep their wording, marked
DRAFT-UNREVISED, because the writing rules govern final text and the
chapter guidance reserves revision of the author's text for when it is
asked for.

**Consequences.** New folder `ch0-introduction/` with `GUIDANCE.md` and
`notes/`; new `manuscript/introduction.qmd`; `_quarto.yml` lists it;
`docs/dissertation-map.md` gains the introduction's framing, the
dependence argument and five hand-off rows; `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`,
`docs/repo-conventions.md` and `docs/execution-path.md` name the new
folder and the new writing track. Fourteen references are listed in the
guidance for the author to add in Zotero before drafting. The next steps
on this track are the author's: the four TODO-develop passages, the term
for the welfare model once Chapter 1 settles it, a writing pass on the
three unrevised paragraphs, and the closing sections from the plan.
