# 2026-10-26 — Baseline covariates on the census anchors, not on the interpolated series

**Context.** The annual panel carries four census-anchored series (working-age population, economically active population, informality, population) interpolated linearly between the 2000, 2010 and 2022 censuses, and ten ratios built on them (the five formal-employment rates over PIA, the four GDP-per-capita columns, transfers per capita and per PEA). Every intercensal cell blends the two surrounding censuses, so a covariate or moderator measured as a pre-treatment mean of one of these series contains the 2010 census, which is post-treatment for the 2008 and 2009 PPCDAm cohorts. The share of 2010 in the blend is fixed by the window: 55 percent for 2004-2007, 45 percent for the 2002-2007 window of `08-data-analysis-2WFE-CS.R`. In the current analysis script, `informal0`, `gdp0`, `formal`, `bf_pc` and `bf_resid` are all such means. Task 1.7 of the rebuild (phase 2, step 2.6) asked that the census values be stored so the analysis can use a clean 2000 value, and the author asked whether the interpolation should be removed altogether in favour of census denominators.

**Decision.** The interpolated annual series stay in the panel, flagged in the codebook (`interpolated`, `anchor_years`). The census values as read are stored in the municipality store `panel_amz_baseline` as `pia_c2000`, `pia_c2010`, `pia_c2022` and likewise for `pea`, `informal` and `population` (D-2026-10-26-a). Rule for every analysis script, under any identification strategy: a covariate, moderator or stratifying variable defined on a pre-treatment window takes its census quantity from the 2000 anchor in the store, never from the interpolated panel series. Concretely, informality at baseline is `informal_c2000`; a baseline employment rate is the pre-treatment mean of the formal-job count divided by `pia_c2000`; baseline transfers or GDP per capita divide the pre-treatment mean of the numerator by `population_c2000`. The interpolated series remain the right object for descriptive trajectories, where the figures already mark the census points, and for time-varying regressors whose use the causal stage must argue separately. Not pursued: replacing the interpolation by predetermined annual series (IBGE's official annual estimates for population, a constant age structure scaled by them for PIA), and a fixed census denominator for the annual series.

**Rationale.** The anchors remove the post-treatment component from every baseline quantity at no cost to the panel: zero cells change. The two alternatives were weighed on the sample. A denominator frozen at the 2000 census overstates a 2009 rate by 30 percent for the median municipality (IQR 16 to 48 percent, a factor of three at the extreme), and the error is correlated with listing because working-age population grew fastest where frontier migration was strongest; a last-census step function avoids that but places a break of the same size in every rate in 2010, one or two years after treatment. Predetermined annual series from IBGE's official estimates are defensible for population (the 2001-2009 estimates use the 2000 census and the state projection only) but differ from the interpolation by 20 percent at the 10th and 90th percentiles by 2006, jump at the 2010 census (median +1.7 percent, tails of -66 and +73 percent) and run 4 to 7 percent above the interpolation by 2021 because the projections overshot the 2022 census; no official annual municipal series by age exists, so an annual PIA without interpolation would itself be a construction. For designs whose covariates are pre-treatment, which is every design on the table, the anchors deliver what the predetermined series would, and the interpolation keeps the true count at both ends for the descriptive use. The author chose the anchors alone on 2026-10-26.

**Consequences.** `02-social-census.R` writes `_data/interim/census_anchors_2000_2022`; `05-sample-definition.R` joins the twelve anchor columns onto the store; the codebook carries the two new columns and the store's rows (spec amended in `rebuild/04-codebook-and-decisions.md`); the `_cYYYY` suffix of the naming convention now covers census anchors as well as Atlas vintages. At the stage 10 rerun, `08-data-analysis-2WFE-CS.R` joins the store and rebuilds its baseline block on the anchors; the formal-job and population counts it needs in the panel arrive with the denominator columns of task 1.8 (step 2.7). The chapter's data section states that baseline covariates use the 2000 census values and that the annual series are interpolated. The predetermined-series alternative can be reopened if a time-varying census-based regressor enters a design; it would be a new phase 2 step with its own decision entry and comparator report.
