# Rules to follow when writing final paragraphs/text

## What to fix when writing/revising final paragraphs / whole text

1. **Grammar and mechanics**
   - Correct grammatical errors, subject-verb agreement, tense consistency,
     and punctuation.
   - Fix run-on sentences and sentence fragments that impede clarity.
   - Use periods and commas as the primary punctuation. Avoid em dashes
     (—), en dashes as connectors, and semicolons unless they are already
     in the original and serve a clear purpose.

2. **Sentence-level revision**
   - Rewrite sentences that are vague, overly long, or structurally
     awkward.
   - Prefer direct constructions: subject + verb + object. Avoid
     unnecessary passive voice.
   - Keep sentence length varied: mix short sentences with medium ones.
     Avoid uniformity.
   - If a sentence can be expressed more plainly without losing meaning,
     use the plainer version.

3. **Paragraph development**
   - Each paragraph must have a clear topic sentence and develop one main
     idea.
   - Transitions between sentences and paragraphs must be smooth and
     logically motivated, not formulaic.
   - Use plain connectors: "and," "but," "so," "because," "since,"
     "although." Avoid overusing "moreover," "furthermore," "additionally,"
     "consequently," "hence," or "thus", especially at the start of a
     sentence.

4. **Coherence and cohesion**
   - The text must read as a continuous, logically connected argument.
   - When referring back to a concept, use the exact same term. Do not
     rotate synonyms to create false variation.
   - Eliminate redundancy: if an idea has been stated, do not restate it as
     a conclusion at the end of the same paragraph or section.

## What to remove: AI writing markers

Replace or delete all of the following without exception:

### Banned vocabulary (replace with plain equivalents)

delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), interplay, intricacies, nuanced
(when used as filler), multifaceted, garner, underscore, showcase, pivotal,
crucial, vital, vibrant, intricate, profound, compelling, testament, align
with, bolstered, enduring, meticulous, meticulously

### Banned phrases and constructions

- "serves as / stands as / acts as / marks" replacing a simple "is"
- "not only X, but also Y" or "it is not just X, it is Y" (negative
  parallelisms)
- "X goes beyond Y" / "X is more than just Y"
- Ending sentences with participial clauses that editorialize:
  "...highlighting its importance," "...reflecting broader trends,"
  "...underscoring its significance," "...contributing to the field"
- Vague attributions without a named source: "experts argue," "scholars
  note," "studies show," "it has been observed"
- Puffery: "plays a vital role," "indelible mark," "lasting legacy,"
  "cannot be overstated," "remains a testament to"
- Promotional language: "nestled in," "boasts a," "rich tapestry of,"
  "vibrant community"
- Formulaic challenge-future structures: "Despite its X, it faces
  challenges... Future prospects include..."
- Three-part rhetorical lists used for effect rather than precision (e.g.,
  "Speed. Scale. Security.")
- Symmetric framing: "On one hand... on the other hand" unless a genuine
  contrast is being drawn
- Conclusions that restate what was just said: "In summary," "In
  conclusion," "To sum up," "As we have seen"
- Didactic disclaimers: "It is important to note," "It is worth
  mentioning," "One must consider"

## What to preserve

- Every argument, finding, and claim in the original text
- All citations and references exactly as written
- The author's voice and disciplinary register
- Intentional stylistic choices that are clearly deliberate, not errors
