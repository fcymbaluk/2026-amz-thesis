### 1.3 Fix price index normalization

`price_index_crop` (~0.014-0.03) and `price_index_cattle` (~26-51) are on incompatible scales and neither equals 1 in 2000, contradicting the documented Pink Sheet spec (MUV-deflated, 2000 = 1). Phase 1 evidence (2026-10-10, `_audit/prices.qmd` Q3-Q4): reproduced in `03-controls-prices.R` (KNOWN DEFECTS); in 2000 the crop index equals the municipality's base-window crop share and the cattle index its base-window density (D-2026-10-10-h). Phase 2, step 2.4 (2026-10-23, `_audit/prices.qmd` Q3-Q7, D-2026-10-23-a/b/c): resolved by reinterpretation, no anchoring. The "2000 = 1" of the specification describes the world price series, which script 03 normalises; the index is the base-window exposure (crop share of the territory, cattle density) times the relative price, so its 2000 value is the exposure itself, absorbed by municipality fixed effects, and the exposure-weighted price change is the shift-share control the design asks for. Anchoring each municipality's index at its 2000 value was implemented on the branch and reversed once tested (Q6): it cancels the exposure, turning the cattle index into the relative beef price in every municipality (no cross-sectional variation, collinear with year effects) and reducing the crop index to crop-mix variation (within-year sd 0.07 against 0.38 between years). The scale difference between the two indices is the unit of the exposures (share versus heads per km²), handled by the z-scores; a pasture-share exposure for cattle would put both on the 0-1 scale but needs a new raw source, the MapBiomas workbook being the deforestation module only (Q7, D-2026-10-23-c, reserved to the author). One genuine defect fixed here: a municipality whose four crop weights are all missing read 0 and now reads NA (Ipiranga do Norte, Itanhangá, Mojuí dos Campos; D-2026-10-23-b). Footprint on the phase 1 panel: `price_index_crop` and `price_index_crop_lag1` for the two MT municipalities (0 to NA, all years) and, through the pool, the two crop z-scores in every cell by a small shift. ## 5. Causal stage

### 5.1 Persistence as the sharper test of mechanism A

Consider whether the persistence question (does compliance hold after enforcement pressure fades) is the sharper test of mechanism A, since sustainability of enforcement is what mechanism A actually claims. Mechanism A (cushioning as compensation) claims that social protection makes enforcement politically sustainable, not only that it makes the initial enforcement shock bite harder. The stratified moderation estimate tests the second; the persistence question tests the first. Present the persistence results as the natural extension of the moderation result rather than as a separate exploration. Keep the existing designs and their labels (system-level preferred, municipality-level descriptive at best), the predetermined 2006-07 moderator, and the price-exposure control. State before estimation what result would count against mechanism A here: a rebound after 2016/2019 that is as steep or steeper in high-coverage municipalities.

### 5.2 Sample gate: rationale, overlap and the main sample

The at-risk cuts (1 and 5 km² per year of 2000-2003 clearing, D-2026-10-05-h) are low relative to the treated group: the smallest baseline clearing among listed municipalities is 71.3 km² in the main window (Mucajaí, RR) and 38.9 km² in the robust window (Senador José Porfírio, PA). The gate is coarse: it removes the clearly irrelevant units and the remaining overlap work falls to the doubly-robust weighting; even the narrow sample leaves 317 controls below the treated propensity range in the main window (`_audit/panel-sample.qmd` Q4, Q6). A cut with a clearer rationale is one set just below the smallest treated baseline clearing, which trims as many non-comparable controls as possible without losing a treated unit (phase 1 count on `panel_amz_baseline`: 73 controls above the floor would remain in the main window and 141 in the robust one, against 357 under the narrow cut). Work: quantify the distance between each cut and the lowest treated municipality under both windows; compare candidate gates on the covariate balance and overlap diagnostics of the notebook, never on estimates (`GUIDANCE.md`: significance is never a design criterion); draw on the survey of task 5.3. State which sample is the main one and why: the analysis script already treats narrow as the main estimation sample (`08-data-analysis-2WFE-CS.R`, `SAMPLE_FLAG <- "at_risk_narrow"`) while the parked script 05 prose presents broad and narrow symmetrically, and the chapter must say plainly that narrow is the main specification and broad the robustness check, or the reverse, and why. An absolute area cut partly selects on municipal size, since large municipalities clear more km² for their territory alone; this is intended, because listing worked the same way, and the covariate side is task 5.4. Any new gate is a definition change in `05-sample-definition.R` with its own decision entry, after the phase 1 gate and before the annex sample section is written (stage 10).

### 5.4 Common support: covariates and the trimming estimand

Two items for the candidate designs in `_scripts/models/`. (a) Covariates: because the area cut selects partly on municipal size, municipal area or forest stock (`munic_area_km2`, `forest_km2_2000` in `panel_amz_baseline`) is a natural covariate alongside baseline deforestation in `xformla`; test it, watching the stability of the doubly-robust step noted in the analysis script. (b) Trimming: listing is highly predictable from four baseline variables (`_audit/panel-sample.qmd` Q6: median propensity about 0.7 for listed municipalities, near 0 for controls; 14 of 43 listed off support in the main window). Listed and non-listed municipalities are not two similar groups that happened to be treated differently; they are structurally different kinds of places: only about 29 of 43 listed units have any comparable control, and only about 46 controls are comparable to any listed unit (legacy diagnostics, to be recomputed on the rebuilt panel). Trimming to common support makes the estimand the effect on the roughly 29 moderately extreme listed municipalities; the 14 dropped are the most intense frontiers, the core PPCDAm targets and probably where enforcement had most to act on. The untrimmed ATT covers the full policy target but depends on extrapolation for the most important units; the trimmed ATT is better identified but answers a narrower question that excludes the places that matter most for the policy. Decide before estimation which estimand is the main one, report both with the dropped units named, and state the trade-off in the chapter in these terms.

### 5.5 Robustness of the price controls to the exposure window and the price base year

The price indices are exposure-weighted relative prices (D-2026-10-23-a): the base-window crop share and cattle density (`WEIGHT_BASE_YEARS`, 2000-2002, `03-controls-agro.R`, D-2026-10-10-f) times the world price relative to `PRICES_BASE` (2000, `03-controls-prices.R`). Both are constants, and the sources cover any window (PAM from 1988, PPM from 1974, Pink Sheet from 1960). Recorded 2026-10-23 at step 2.4 as a robustness check for the models, not a pipeline change. (a) Exposure window: re-estimate the main specifications with weights from an alternative window, for instance 2002 alone, 2004 alone or 2002-2004; any window must stay predetermined relative to the treatment (up to 2007 for the 2008 listing cohort; PPCDAm starts in 2004, so a window ending in 2003 is the latest clean choice for the policy as a whole). A later window would also give the municipalities installed in 2005 (Ipiranga do Norte, Itanhangá) weights of their own, at the cost of being post-policy. (b) Price base year: the base rescales each commodity by a different constant and therefore re-weights the crops (the effective weight of a crop is its area share times the ratio of its 2000 price to its base-year price), so the base is a statement about the prices at which the exposure is valued; align it with the exposure window whenever that window moves (a base-window average price is the consistent choice). Implementation: alternative weights as a second interim file named by its window (`pam_ppm_weights_base_YYYY_YYYY`), the alternative indices built in `_scripts/models/` or `robustness/`, never by changing the main constants; the main window stays 2000-2002 with 2000 as the price base unless a decision entry changes the definition. Report the sensitivity of the treatment estimates, not of the price coefficients, since the indices are controls.
